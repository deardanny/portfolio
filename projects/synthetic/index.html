Carnegie Mellon University · A/B testing study

Human vs. LLM survey responses

Examining what changes when synthetic respondents dominate an experiment.

The question

What can an experiment tell us when most survey responses are generated by an LLM rather than collected from people?

Approach

Our team tested a “Skip Ad with Premium” interface concept using a randomized survey. We analyzed 38 human responses separately from 1,100 Claude-generated responses, using OLS with robust standard errors, treatment-by-source interactions, and power analysis. Splitting the sources exposed a pattern hidden by the pooled estimate.

My contribution

Worked on average treatment effects, subgroup comparisons, presentation structure, and limitations. I helped frame the difference between matching respondents’ demographics and reproducing how people react to a treatment.

Results & evidence

On stated willingness to click Premium, the reported estimate was +0.011 Likert points for humans and +0.912 for synthetic respondents. These are estimates of stated intent, not observed clicks, paid subscriptions, or a real YouTube deployment.

Scope & interpretation

Synthetic-generation instructions included a treatment-direction cue, so the contrast is not an unbiased test of general LLM behavior. The human sample is too small to distinguish no effect from a modest effect. The pooled estimate is exploratory, not a validated upper bound on the human effect.

What this work taught me

Synthetic data can make an estimate look precise without making it valid. Generation assumptions belong beside the result.
