Method & project evidence Open the supporting figure

Approach

The team combined county-level outage histories with weather features and compared persistence and historical baselines, SARIMAX, a Seq2Seq LSTM, and XGBoost. Lagged and rolling features capture recent conditions; chronological evaluation reflects the forward-looking task. Forecasts feed county prioritization under generator capacity constraints.

My contribution

Implemented forecasting models and analysis for the team project, and helped translate model comparison into a placement recommendation. My work connected data preparation, evaluation, and the final report rather than ending at a prediction table.

Results & evidence

Deliverables include a multi-model notebook, 24-hour and 48-hour prediction tasks across 83 counties, and a recommendation for five generators. Each generator is assumed to serve up to 1,000 households and cannot be relocated once deployed.

Scope & interpretation

The study uses an April–June 2023 window and does not establish performance across seasons. Weather availability differs between retrospective validation and the final scenario; the report’s final XGBoost forecasts are flat within each county. This page emphasizes the decision framework rather than operational accuracy.

What this work taught me

Make the downstream resource constraint explicit—and distinguish a retrospective benchmark from a deployable forecast.
