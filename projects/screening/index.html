Method & project evidence Open the supporting figure

Approach

I translated a literature-review screening protocol into an LLM classification workflow, then compared its inclusion decisions with human-assigned labels. Cohen’s kappa makes agreement interpretable beyond raw matches by accounting for agreement expected by chance.

My contribution

Developed the screening classifier and evaluation workflow alongside my work as a human screener. I used disagreements and model-reported confidence to identify cases needing closer manual review, connecting model evaluation to the actual research process.

Results & evidence

The classifier reached approximately 0.80 Cohen’s kappa against human-assigned labels. The outcome is a way to inspect consistency and direct reviewer attention, rather than treating model output as a final screening authority.

Scope & interpretation

Agreement is not correctness: human and model labels can agree and still be wrong. Low-confidence decisions are routed for manual review, and confidence needs continued validation as the document mix changes.

What this work taught me

Evaluation should determine where automation belongs in a workflow, not simply produce a headline score.
