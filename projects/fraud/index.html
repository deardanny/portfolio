Method & project evidence Open the supporting figure

Approach

We examined imbalanced claims, compared classifiers, and investigated identifiers carrying misleading predictive signal. The analysis contrasts an F1-oriented threshold with a cost objective assigning different penalties to missed fraud and false-alarm investigations.

My contribution

Co-developed the analysis in a two-person team. I worked on evaluation helpers and cost-based threshold analysis, making the consequences of each error explicit rather than selecting a model on accuracy alone.

Results & evidence

The notebook demonstrates identifier-leakage diagnosis, precision–recall analysis, model comparisons, and cost sensitivity. The assumptions are $15,000 per missed fraudulent claim and $500 per false alarm; these are modeling assumptions, not measured business losses.

Scope & interpretation

The final cost threshold was chosen on the same held-out set used to report its outcome. Reported recall and savings are therefore exploratory, not independent out-of-sample estimates. A deployment-quality evaluation would choose the threshold on training or validation predictions, then assess it once on an untouched test set.

What this work taught me

A striking metric is less valuable than understanding how evaluation choices produced it.
