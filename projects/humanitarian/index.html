Humanitarian Efficiency Radar

Making humanitarian budget anomalies explainable and practical to review.

Completed datathonRegressionAnomaly detectionPlotlyDatabricks
My role
Problem framing, communication & notebook assembly
Scope
UN pooled-fund project budgets
When
January 2026

The question

Which aid-project budgets look unusual relative to comparable projects, and how can a small review team focus its attention?

Approach

Our team benchmarked projects within fund-and-sector peer groups. A log-budget versus log-beneficiary regression produces residuals; within-group Z-scores identify unusually high or low budgets. Plotly views support exploring flagged cases and their context.

My contribution

Contributed the executive summary, problem framing, and final notebook assembly. I helped communicate why a statistical flag should trigger review rather than be treated as a verdict, and connected the analysis to an audit-capacity constraint.

Results & evidence

The final notebook reports 123 flagged projects among 15,664 analyzed projects—approximately 0.79%. The output is a short review list with a peer-relative explanation, not a finding that those projects were wasteful.

Scope & interpretation

Exceptional logistics, data-entry problems, or legitimate operating constraints can produce anomalies. A secondary classifier uses labels derived from the outlier rule; its score is not independently validated fraud-detection accuracy. Flagged budget exposure is not realized savings.

What this work taught me

Give reviewers a manageable list and a defensible reason to look closer.

Team-method summary and aggregate notebook counts; the source repository remains private.
