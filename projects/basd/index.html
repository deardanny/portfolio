All school labels, rubric details, example text and displayed scores are invented. This preview explains the aggregate reporting workflow without disclosing district records.

Method & project evidence Open the supporting figure

Approach

I standardized records from inconsistent templates, separated structured scores from free-text evidence, and used Claude Haiku and Sonnet for keyword and theme extraction. Before analysis, spaCy named-entity recognition and rules masked identifying information; identity mappings were maintained separately.

My contribution

Built preparation, masking, and analysis workflows and developed Power BI views for school-level comparisons. I connected the pipeline to the district’s evaluation criteria and presented findings to inform training and support priorities.

Results & evidence

The pipeline standardized more than 50,000 evaluation records and analyzed over 25,000 qualitative comments. The lowest-scoring evaluation area also had the most missing supporting evidence, giving the district a specific pattern to investigate.

Scope & interpretation

Records and comments are different units. The finding is an association, not evidence that missing comments caused lower scores. This case study contains no individual teacher evaluations.

What this work taught me

Privacy handling and the meaning of an evaluation rubric are central design decisions, not finishing steps.
